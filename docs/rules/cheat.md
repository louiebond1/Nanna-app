# Cheat: house rules (two players)

Cheat (also called "I Doubt It") is a traditional public-domain card game.

- **Deal:** one 52-card deck, 26 cards each. The starting player is chosen at random by the server.
- **The rank to call:** it starts at Aces and goes up one each turn: Aces, Twos, … Kings, then Aces again. It's always shown on screen.
- **Your turn:** put 1 to 4 cards face down and say they're that many of the current rank. The *number* of cards is always truthful, because it's visible. The *rank* may be a bluff.
- **Responding:** the other player either:
  - **believes** you: the cards stay in the middle pile; or
  - **calls "Cheat!"**: the cards just played are turned over for both players to see.
    - If any of them isn't the called rank, the bluffer picks up the whole pile.
    - If they were all honest, the caller picks up the whole pile.
- **Next turn:** play always passes to the player who just responded, with the next rank. That's normal two-player alternation.
- **Winning:** you win when you put down your last cards and either the other player believes you, or they call "Cheat!" and you were honest. If you were caught bluffing on your last cards, you pick up the pile and play on.
- **Help:** the screen tells you how many of the required rank you hold (a hint), and explains what calling will do.
- **Privacy:** cards in the pile are never sent to the other device unless a call turns them over. Then they're shown by name in the move log.
